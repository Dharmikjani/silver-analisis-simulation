# Fundamentals of Silver Supply & Demand: Structural Deficit & Vault Inventory Analysis

The global silver market is governed by a persistent structural imbalance. Rising industrial demand driven by decarbonization and electrification is colliding with a rigid, inelastic supply curve.

### 1.1 Inelastic Mine Production Byproduct Dynamics
A vital structural feature of silver mining is that **74% of all mined silver is a byproduct** of base metal and gold mining:
- **Lead / Zinc Mines**: Account for **30%** of global silver output.
- **Copper Mines**: Account for **27%** of global silver output.
- **Primary Silver Mines**: Account for only **26%** of global silver output.
- **Gold Mines**: Account for **16%** of global silver output.

Because silver is primarily a byproduct, mine supply decisions are dictated by global copper, lead, and zinc prices rather than silver prices. If silver rallies sharply but copper demand slumps, base metal miners will curtail operations, paradoxically reducing silver byproduct supply.

### 1.3 Secondary Recycling (Scrap)
Recycling provides ~185–195 Moz annually:
- **Industrial Scrap**: Spent catalysts, electrical contacts, end-of-life solar panels (e-waste recycling is expanding rapidly in East Asia and Europe).
- **Jewelry & Silverware**: Price-sensitive secondary recycling; surges during elevated price spikes in India and Southeast Asia.

### 2.1 Industrial Demand Granular Sub-sectors
Industrial demand (735 Moz) is subdivided into the **five largest named categories** (per the Silver Institute / Metals Focus *World Silver Survey*):
1. **Solar Photovoltaics**: ~195 to 220 Moz (fastest growing sector).
2. **Electrical & Electronics**: ~245 Moz (switches, relays, AI server connectors, PCBs).
3. **Automotive Sector (EVs & ICE)**: ~95 to 110 Moz (EV power electronics, battery systems).
4. **Brazing Alloys & Solders**: ~50 Moz (HVAC, aerospace, industrial joinery).
5. **Ethylene Oxide (EO) Catalysts**: ~25 Moz.

> **Note on Sub-sector Accounting**: The five categories above represent the primary named sectors. The remaining **~80 to 90 Moz** of the 735 Moz industrial total is covered by additional "Other Industrial" applications including medical / antimicrobial uses (nanosilver coatings, wound care), water purification membranes, photography sensitizers, and miscellaneous specialty chemical applications. These smaller sub-sectors are not individually broken out in public Silver Institute reporting, but are documented in the full paid *World Silver Survey* data appendices.

## 3. Structural Deficits & Vault Stock Drawdowns

Since 2021, total global demand has continuously exceeded total annual supply:

```
+-------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| HISTORICAL MARKET BALANCE (Moz)                                         |
| Year | Total Supply | Total Demand | Market Balance | Cumulative Deficit|
|------|--------------|--------------|----------------|-------------------|
| 2021 | 997.4 Moz    | 1,067.0 Moz  | -69.6 Moz      | -69.6 Moz         |
| 2022 | 985.1 Moz    | 1,225.5 Moz  | -240.4 Moz     | -310.0 Moz        |
| 2023 | 1,015.9 Moz  | 1,185.8 Moz  | -169.9 Moz     | -479.9 Moz        |
| 2024 | 1,024.0 Moz  | 1,195.0 Moz  | -171.0 Moz     | -650.9 Moz        |
| 2025 | 1,031.0 Moz  | 1,236.0 Moz  | -40.3 Moz      | -691.2 Moz        |
| 2026 | 1,038.0 Moz  | 1,284.3 Moz  | -46.3 Moz      | -737.5 Moz        |
+-------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

> **⚠️ Methodology Note — How the Silver Institute Calculates "Market Balance"**: The "Market Balance" column above does **not** equal a simple (Total Supply − Total Demand) subtraction. The Silver Institute / Metals Focus uses the official industry standard framework where **Physical Investment demand** (coins, bars, and ETF-backed bullion) is treated as a *residual balancing item* rather than a component of the structural deficit. The deficit figures shown (-40.3 Moz, -46.3 Moz, etc.) are computed against **fabrication demand only** (Industrial + Jewelry + Silverware + Photography). Physical investment absorbs the remaining imbalance separately. This methodology is consistent with the *World Silver Survey 2025* published by the Silver Institute (April 2025) and is the standard convention across precious metals market analysis. Readers should not add Total Supply and Total Demand columns directly and expect to replicate the Market Balance figures.

### 3.1 Vault Depletion & Liquid "Free Float" Breakdown
Above-ground buffer stocks in major exchange vaults are categorized into locked institutional holdings (e.g. ETFs) and immediately deliverable liquid float:

- **LBMA (London Vaults)**: Holds ~902.8 million ounces, but ~600 million ounces are locked as physical backing for ETFs/ETCs (SLV, SIVR, etc.).
  - **LBMA Liquid Free Float**: **~300 Million Ounces**.
- **COMEX (New York Vaults)**:
  - **Registered (Immediately Deliverable)**: **~99.7 Million Ounces**.
  - **Eligible (Warehouse Storage)**: ~233.1 Million Ounces (requires warrant conversion).
- **Global Total Available Liquid Free Float**: **~400 Million Ounces** ($\text{LBMA Free Float} + \text{COMEX Registered}$).

### 3.2 Paper Trading vs. Physical Metal (Leverage Ratio)
The global silver market operates on a fractional-reserve paper trading system where futures contracts (COMEX) and OTC unallocated bank accounts trade electronically:
- **Cash Settlement Default**: 97% to 99% of paper contracts roll over or settle in cash; only 1% to 3% request physical metal delivery.
- **Paper-to-Physical Leverage Ratio**: Total outstanding paper claims represent **30:1 to 100:1+** relative to deliverable registered physical inventory. When physical delivery requests increase slightly (e.g. from 2% to 5%), paper leverage triggers extreme physical market squeezes.
