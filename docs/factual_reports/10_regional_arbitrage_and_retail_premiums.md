## 1. Global Exchange Price Divergence: Shanghai (SGE) vs London (LBMA) vs US (COMEX)

Precious metals pricing exhibits significant geographic divergence between paper-dominated Western futures exchanges (COMEX/LBMA) and physical-delivery-dominated Asian exchanges (Shanghai Gold Exchange - SGE & Shanghai Futures Exchange - SHFE).

```
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### Key Regional Arbitrage Dynamics:
1. **Persistent Shanghai Premium (+8% to +14%)**: The Shanghai Gold Exchange has maintained a structural premium over London spot due to massive domestic consumption by solar cell manufacturers (TOPCon/HJT paste producers) and Chinese retail investment.
2. **West-to-East Physical Drain**: Arbitrage traders buy physical silver in London (LBMA) at $58.00/oz, ship it to China, and sell it on the SGE at $63.80/oz. This physical drain permanently removes metal from Western LBMA/COMEX vaults.
3. **China Export Restriction Barrier**: Once silver enters China, strict export licensing prevents the physical metal from easily flowing back to Western markets, creating a one-way physical sink.

## 4. Sensitivity Impact on Western Vault Free Float & Price Models

Per the **Dynamic Price Impact Rule**, we evaluate how regional arbitrage and Indian import volumes impact our overall market balance models:

1. **West-to-East Vault Drainage**:
   - Combined physical demand from China (SGE) and India absorbs over **500 Million Ounces annually** (~50% of global total supply).
   - This physical flow drains **120–160 Moz per year** directly out of LBMA London vaults.
2. **Impact on Free Float Depletion**:
   - Because silver shipped to India and China is locked away in domestic private holdings, jewelry, and solar cells, it does not return to Western vaults.
   - This one-way physical drain confirms our accelerated **Late 2027 – Early 2028 Free Float Crunch Window**.
3. **Price Floor Support**:
   - High physical premiums in Shanghai ($63.80/oz) and India ($64.90/oz) establish a strong physical price arbitrage floor, preventing paper short sellers from driving spot prices below **$50.00/oz**.
