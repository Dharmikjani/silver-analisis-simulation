## 2. Mexico Mining Law Reforms & Open-Pit Mining Ban

Mexico's federal legislative reforms have introduced structural hurdles for primary precious metals miners:

1. **Constitutional Open-Pit Mining Ban**: New open-pit mine development concessions have been halted. Open-pit operations (e.g., Newmont's Peñasquito mine producing ~31.0 Moz Ag/yr) face terminal reserve lives without open-pit expansion approvals.
2. **Water Extraction Concession Limits**: The National Water Commission (CONAGUA) has tightened industrial water permits in arid mining states (Zacatecas, Durango, Sonora), forcing miners to invest heavily in water recycling infrastructure.
3. **Exploration Concession Monopoly**: Exploration rights have been reserved exclusively for the Mexican Geological Service (SGM), restricting private exploration by foreign juniors and majors (Fresnillo, Pan American, First Majestic).

> [!WARNING]
> **Mexican Mine Output Drag Projection**: Regulatory delays and open-pit restrictions are projected to cause a **-25.0 to -35.0 Moz/year reduction** in Mexican silver mine output over the 2026–2030 period.

### Strategic Implications of US Critical Mineral Status:
- **US Inflation Reduction Act (IRA Section 45X & 30D)**: Solar module manufacturers and EV battery developers receive direct tax credits for sourcing silver from domestic or US Free Trade Agreement (FTA) partner countries (e.g., Canada, Mexico, Chile, Australia).
- **Tariff Protection**: Non-FTA silver imports face potential tariffs or supply chain tracing requirements, creating a dual-tier market between FTA-compliant silver and non-compliant silver.

### Summary Conclusion:
- Geopolitical risks in Latin America create an asymmetrical **upward price risk**. 
- Because 74% of silver is a secondary byproduct of copper, lead, and zinc, geopolitical disruptions in base metal mines automatically curtail silver supply, exacerbating physical deficits.
