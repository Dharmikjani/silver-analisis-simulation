## 2. E-Waste Urban Mining Economics & Processing Technologies

Extracting silver from electronic waste (WEEE - Waste Electrical and Electronic Equipment) involves high technological and logistical complexity.

```
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| E-WASTE RECYCLING TECHNOLOGY & COST COMPARISON                                                                        |
| Technology Process             | Recovery Efficiency (%) | Processing Cost ($/oz Ag) | Key Bottleneck & Limitation               |
|--------------------------------|-------------------------|---------------------------|-------------------------------------------|
| **Pyrometallurgical Smelting** | 98.2%                   | $18.20 / oz               | High CAPEX energy smelters (Umicore/Boliden)|
| **Hydrometallurgical Leaching**| 94.5%                   | $14.50 / oz               | Eco-toxic chemical waste disposal limits  |
| **Bio-Leaching (Advanced)**    | 88.0%                   | $11.00 / oz               | Slow reaction kinetics (long leaching time)|
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### Urban Mining Collection Bottlenecks:
1. **Low Unit Intensity**: A typical smartphone contains only **250 to 350 mg of silver**. Processing 1 metric tonne of smartphones yields ~$600 in silver, making collection and manual sorting logistics the primary cost driver.
2. **Industrial EO Catalyst Recycling**: Chemical plants using Ethylene Oxide (EO) catalysts utilize thousands of ounces of pure silver per reactor vessel. These catalysts are continuously recycled under closed-loop corporate contracts (99%+ recovery rate), leaving little room for uncaptured supply upside.

## 4. Deficit Cushioning & Price Ceiling Impact

Per the **Dynamic Price Impact Rule**, we evaluate whether secondary recycling can cap silver price increases or eliminate market deficits:

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Can Recycling Plug the Deficit?**
> **NO.** Even under an extreme price surge to **$85.00–$100.00/oz**, secondary recycling supply caps out at **~275 Moz/year** (+82 Moz above baseline). 
> Because structural annual deficits are projected at **-115 to -185 Moz/year** by 2028–2030, secondary recycling can only cushion **30% to 45%** of the physical deficit gap.

### Quantitative Summary:
- Secondary recycling acts as a **dampener on violent price spikes**, but **cannot stop above-ground vault inventory drawdown**.
- The structural inelasticity of primary mine supply (74% byproduct) combined with the physical ceiling on scrap recycling confirms that **Vault Free Float Depletion remains inevitable by Late 2027 – Early 2028**.
