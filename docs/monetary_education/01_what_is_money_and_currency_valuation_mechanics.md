## 3. How Are Currencies Valued & Exchanged Today?

If no currency is backed by gold, why is $1 USD worth ~85 Indian Rupees, or ~0.92 Euros, or ~7.2 Chinese Yuan? 

On global foreign exchange (FX) markets ($7.5 Trillion traded daily), fiat currencies are priced relative to one another based on **6 fundamental forces**:

### A. Relative Supply Expansion (M2 Money Supply Growth)
* If Country A increases its total money supply by 15% in a year, while Country B increases its money supply by only 4%, Country A's currency will naturally depreciate against Country B's currency over the medium term.

### B. Interest Rate Differentials & The "Carry Trade"
* Capital flows globally seeking yield. If the US Federal Reserve sets interest rates at 5.0% while the Bank of Japan sets rates at 0.25%, global investors borrow Yen at low cost and convert it into US Dollars to earn 5.0% yield in US Treasuries. This creates massive demand for USD, driving the Dollar higher relative to the Yen.

### C. Purchasing Power Parity (PPP)
* The theoretical law of one price: Over long cycles, exchange rates should adjust so that an identical basket of goods (e.g., the *Big Mac Index*) costs approximately the same in both countries when converted to a common currency.

### D. Trade Balance & Current Account Balance
* If a country imports vastly more goods than it exports (running massive trade deficits like the US), it constantly floods foreign markets with its currency to pay for those imports. Foreign nations must choose to reinvest those currency surpluses back into that country's sovereign bonds or assets.

### E. The Sovereign Debt-to-GDP Ratio & Fiscal Deficits
* When a government runs chronic budget deficits exceeding 6–8% of GDP and its national debt climbs past 100–120% of GDP, foreign investors begin demanding higher risk premiums, fearing future currency debasement (inflationary default).

### F. Geopolitical Network Effects (The Reserve Currency Privilege)
* For 80 years, the US Dollar has enjoyed the **"Exorbitant Privilege"**:
  1. **Global Trade Settlement:** Over 85% of global foreign trade and commodities (crude oil, copper, grains) have historically been priced and settled in USD.
  2. **Global Central Bank FX Reserves:** Over 58% of all foreign exchange reserves held by global central banks are denominated in US Dollars and US Treasuries.
  3. **SWIFT Monopoly:** The global interbank messaging clearing system is dominated by Western financial institutions.

Contrary to popular belief, **governments do not print most money; commercial banks create it through lending.**

### The Inescapable Mathematical Trap of Fiat:
Because all money is created as **Principal Debt**, but the **Interest** to repay that debt was never created in the initial loan, the total debt in a fiat economy is **always greater than the total money in existence**.

Therefore, to prevent mass bankruptcies and systemic default, the banking system and central banks **MUST continuously expand total debt and money supply every single year**. 

* **If money printing stops:** The debt pyramid collapses into an acute deflationary depression.
* **If money printing accelerates:** The purchasing power of the currency collapses into high inflation.

## 5. The Cantillon Effect: The Hidden Wealth Transfer

Named after 18th-century economist **Richard Cantillon**, this is the most critical socio-economic mechanism of fiat currency creation:

> [!CAUTION]
> **The Cantillon Principle**:
> When a central bank creates trillions in new fiat currency, that money does NOT distribute evenly to everyone simultaneously. 
> 
> 1. **The First Receivers** (Government agencies, Primary Dealer Wall Street banks, defense contractors, mega-corporations) receive the new money **BEFORE** prices and wages in the broader economy have adjusted. They buy real estate, stocks, and commodities at uninflated prices.
> 2. **The Last Receivers** (Average salaried workers, gig workers, small business owners, pensioners) receive the new money months or years later through slow wage adjustments—**AFTER** housing, groceries, energy, and medical costs have already skyrocketed.
> 
> **Result:** Fiat expansion is a stealth mechanism that transfers real purchasing power from the middle class and wage earners to the financial elite and asset owners.
