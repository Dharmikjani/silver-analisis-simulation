# 🇮🇳 India & The RBI: Money Supply Expansion, M3 Growth & Rupee Purchasing Power

## Executive Summary

In India, the **Reserve Bank of India (RBI)** along with the commercial banking system expands the total broad money supply (**M3**) at an average rate of **10.5% to 14.5% per year**.

Over the last 25 years (2000 to 2026), India's total money supply expanded from **₹10.5 Lakh Crore to over ₹255 Lakh Crore**—a **24-fold increase (2,300%+ growth)**.

This report breaks down the exact annual growth rates, how RBI and Indian commercial banks create this money, and the mathematical proof of why gold, silver, and real estate prices in India have exploded in Rupee terms.

## 2. How is Money Created in India? (RBI vs. Commercial Banks)

Many people believe RBI physically prints paper currency notes at presses (like Dewas, Nashik, Salboni, Mysuru). But physical notes are only a small fraction (~12–14%) of India's money!

### A. The 2-Tier Money Creation Engine:

```
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                             HOW MONEY IS CREATED IN INDIA                                │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│                                                                                          │
│  1. The RBI (Reserve Bank of India) — Creates Base Money (M0) (~14% of Total)            │
│     - Buys Government Bonds (G-Secs) through Open Market Operations (OMO).               │
│     - Buys Foreign Currency (US Dollars) and injects fresh Indian Rupees.                │
│     - Sets the Repo Rate, Cash Reserve Ratio (CRR), and Statutory Liquidity Ratio (SLR). │
│                                                                                          │
│  2. Commercial Banks (SBI, HDFC, ICICI, etc.) — Creates Broad Money (M3) (~86% of Total) │
│     - When a customer deposits ₹1,00,000 in a bank:                                      │
│     - Bank keeps ₹4,500 (4.5% CRR) with RBI and ~18% in SLR (Govt Bonds).                │
│     - Bank lends out the remaining ~₹77,500 as home loans, car loans, business loans.    │
│     - That ₹77,500 gets deposited in another bank account and is lent out AGAIN.         │
│                                                                                          │
│  ► Through the "Money Multiplier" (approx 5.5x in India), ₹1 of RBI Base Money turns     │
│    into ~₹5.50 of total spending money in the Indian economy!                            │
│                                                                                          │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

## 3. The Mathematics: Why Indian Gold & Silver Prices Keep Rising

To understand why gold and silver in India rise faster than in the US, look at the **Macroeconomic Formula of Currency Depreciation**:

$$\text{True Inflation / Currency Dilution} = \text{M3 Money Supply Growth Rate} - \text{Real GDP Growth Rate}$$

In India:
* **Average M3 Growth:** $\approx 11.5\% - 13.0\%$ per year.
* **Average Real GDP Growth:** $\approx 6.5\% - 7.0\%$ per year.
* **Net Excess Currency Dilution:** $\approx 12.0\% - 6.8\% = \mathbf{+5.2\%\text{ to }+6.0\%\text{ per year}}.$
